# 04 · Repair Arm

:::note For the teacher
Review the **Implementation** tab first (phases and concepts), prepare the **Resources** (base
program and guide), then close out planning with **CNB & Standards** and **Evaluation**. You can
rotate team roles — Builder, Organizer, Programmer — throughout the module.
:::

<Module id="m4">
  <Module.Question>
    How can a robot help repair a water pipe without putting a person at risk?
  </Module.Question>

  <Module.Context>
    The mission simulates repairing water infrastructure in a community. It introduces a color
    sensor, reference marks, a manipulator arm, gripping, and part replacement.
  </Module.Context>

  <Module.Concepts items={[
    "Color sensor",
    "Black mark as a signal",
    "Manipulator and gripper",
    "Urban maintenance",
    "Collaborative work between robots",
  ]} />

  <Module.Phase kind="act" label="Activate · 12 min" title="Water infrastructure">
    Talk about pipes, drinking water, and repair risks in narrow or dangerous spaces. Each team
    defines an inspection zone, a damaged part, and a replacement part. Initial evidence: a simple
    problem diagram and one technical reason to send a robot instead of a person.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="exp" label="Explore · 25 min" title="Detection and arm">
    Review the color sensor, the arm motor, and the gripper. Take readings from black, white, and
    nearby colors before programming, and test the arm slowly without putting fingers inside the
    gripper. Checkpoint: the team chooses a color threshold or condition, explains the grip
    position, and predicts which reading could cause a false detection.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="cre" label="Create · 70-100 min" title="Remove and replace">
    Build the Repair Arm and program the sequence as states: move forward to the black mark, stop,
    lower the arm, pick up the damaged part, back up, and place the new part. Record a photo or note
    for each attempt: detected the mark, picked up the part, did not drop it, and placed it in
    position. If it fails, debug the sensor threshold, gripper alignment, closing time, and reverse
    distance separately.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="ref" label="Reflect · 15 min" title="Sensors as decisions">
    Compare moving for a set time with moving until a mark is detected. Each group justifies which
    solution handles changes in distance or lighting better and submits one concrete improvement for
    the next test. To scale the challenge, 1st básico compares colors, 2nd básico tunes the
    threshold with data, and 3rd básico explains why sensor-based decisions make the repair safer.
  </Module.Phase>
