# 03 · Forklift

:::note For the teacher
Review the **Implementation** tab first (phases and concepts), prepare the **Resources** (base
program and guide), then close out planning with **CNB & Standards** and **Evaluation**. You can
rotate team roles — Builder, Organizer, Programmer — throughout the module.
:::

<Module id="m3">
  <Module.Question>
    How does a robot move a load in a market, warehouse, or workshop without crashing or losing
    precision?
  </Module.Question>

  <Module.Context>
    Students build a forklift inspired by local warehouses, markets, and workshops. The module
    works with centimeter-level movement, controlled turns, motorized lifting, and repeatable
    maneuvers.
  </Module.Context>

  <Module.Concepts items={[
    "Maneuverability",
    "Distance-based movement",
    "Rack and pinion",
    "Load lifting",
    "Precision testing",
  ]} />

  <Module.Phase kind="act" label="Activate · 12 min" title="Warehouses and logistics">
    Show images or describe a supermarket, market, or warehouse and ask teams to draw an aisle with
    a pickup zone and a delivery zone. The teacher focuses the question: what happens if the
    forklift approaches too fast or lifts the load to the wrong height? Initial evidence: marked
    route, container position, and a success criterion in centimeters.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="exp" label="Explore · 25 min" title="Chassis and forks">
    Identify the drive motors and the manipulator motor. Review how the rack converts rotation into
    vertical movement and test three fork heights without a load. Checkpoint: the team moves the
    forks without touching the rack with their fingers, explains which height picks up the container
    best, and notes what could make the load fall.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="cre" label="Create · 70-100 min" title="Pick up and place a container">
    Build the Forklift and program the mission in segments: measured approach, lift, back up, turn,
    and place the container in a marked zone. After each attempt, measure the distance between the
    container and target, record the adjustment, and repeat. If it fails, isolate the issue: fork
    height, approach distance, turn angle, floor friction, or speed while carrying the load.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="ref" label="Reflect · 15 min" title="Precision isn't luck">
    Measure how far the load ended up from the target and share a table of attempt, error, and
    adjustment. The team explains which change improved the result the most and which change did not
    matter. To scale the challenge, 1st básico identifies fork positions, 2nd básico calculates
    average error, and 3rd básico optimizes the sequence to repeat three deliveries with the least
    variation.
  </Module.Phase>
