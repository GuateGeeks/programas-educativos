# 06 · Compact Loader

:::note For the teacher
Review the **Implementation** tab first (phases and concepts), prepare the **Resources** (base
program and guide), then close out planning with **CNB & Standards** and **Evaluation**. You can
rotate team roles — Builder, Organizer, Programmer — throughout the module.
:::

<Module id="m6">
  <Module.Question>
    How does a robot hold its heading when the terrain is uneven, like a street under repair or a
    slope?
  </Module.Question>

  <Module.Context>
    The GuateGeeks mini-loader introduces orientation control using yaw/gyroscope. It works with
    corrected straight-line movement, 45° turns, and a front blade for small urban or community
    tasks.
  </Module.Context>

  <Module.Concepts items={[
    "Yaw and orientation",
    "Trajectory correction",
    "Blade and arm",
    "Center of gravity",
    "Movement in the city",
  ]} />

  <Module.Phase kind="act" label="Activate · 12 min" title="Small machines, big problems">
    List urban tasks where a large machine gets in the way: sidewalks, gardening, dirt, debris, or
    minor repairs. Each team marks a short route with a light object for the blade to move and
    predicts where the robot might drift. Initial evidence: expected path sketch and a success
    criterion for measuring drift.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="exp" label="Explore · 25 min" title="Hub orientation">
    Explain that the hub can detect whether it turned and that yaw must be reset before a test. Run
    two paths without the blade task: one time-based and one with heading correction, then measure
    how much each one drifts. Checkpoint: the team identifies the correct hub orientation, resets
    yaw, and keeps hands away from the blade while the robot moves.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="cre" label="Create · 70-100 min" title="Route with the blade">
    Build the Compact Loader and program it to hold its heading, move forward a distance, turn 45
    degrees, and push or carry a light object. Record expected path, actual path, final angle, and
    adjustment made. If it fails, debug yaw reset, hub orientation, floor friction, jammed object, or
    blade speed before changing the whole program.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="ref" label="Reflect · 15 min" title="Feedback-based control">
    Compare a time-based program with one that watches orientation and decide which handles uneven
    terrain errors better. Each team shares one drift measurement and one improvement they would
    apply on an irregular street. To scale the challenge, 1st básico observes the difference between
    paths, 2nd básico calculates average drift, and 3rd básico tunes the correction logic with data.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Cnb items={[

  ]} />
