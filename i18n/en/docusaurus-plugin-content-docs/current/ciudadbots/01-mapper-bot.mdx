# 01 · Mapper Bot

:::note For the teacher
Review the **Implementation** tab first (phases and concepts), prepare the **Resources** (base
program and guide), then close out planning with **CNB & Standards** and **Evaluation**. You can
rotate team roles — Builder, Organizer, Programmer — throughout the module.
:::

<Module id="m1">
  <Module.Question>
    How can a robot draw a neighborhood's blueprint by following exact instructions?
  </Module.Question>

  <Module.Context>
    Students build a GuateGeeks mapping robot that draws on paper. The challenge connects
    neighborhood maps — streets, houses, markets, and services — with distance-based movement, 90°
    and 45° turns, loops, and physically calibrating the marker.
  </Module.Context>

  <Module.Concepts items={[
    "Sequential algorithm",
    "Distance via wheel rotation",
    "90° and 45° turns",
    "Loops for geometric shapes",
    "Calibration and testing",
  ]} />

  <Module.Phase kind="act" label="Activate · 15 min" title="City blueprints">
    Show a simple community map and ask each team to mark the origin, destination, streets, and
    services that should appear. The teacher helps turn the drawing into measurable instructions:
    distance, turn, and repetition. Initial evidence: a sketch with approximate scale and a success
    criterion for deciding whether the robot drew the expected blueprint.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="exp" label="Explore · 25 min" title="Designing the drawing robot">
    Identify two motors, a distance sensor, wheels, a caster ball, and a marker. Test the turn with
    the robot lifted and check that the marker sits on the turning axis; if it is offset, the drawing
    will drift. Checkpoint: the team explains which part controls distance, which part controls
    turning, and how they will remove the marker before handling motors or wheels.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="cre" label="Create · 60-90 min" title="Build, program, and calibrate">
    Build the Mapper Bot with the visual guide and download the base program. First run a minimum
    route: move forward 5 cm and turn 90 degrees; record actual distance, error, and likely cause.
    Once motion is repeatable, use a loop to draw a square and extend it into a house with a roof.
    If the trace warps, decide whether the issue comes from the wheel, marker, surface, or turn
    block.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="ref" label="Reflect · 15 min" title="From error to precision">
    Compare drawings between teams and classify each difference as mechanical, mathematical, or
    program-related. Each team submits brief evidence: first drawing, final drawing, and the
    adjustment that reduced error the most. To scale the challenge, 1st básico traces guided shapes,
    2nd básico calculates average error, and 3rd básico redesigns the route to represent more
    community features.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Cnb items={[

    "Communication and Language, Spanish: oral or written explanation of the algorithm, adjustments, and relationship between instructions and result.",
    "Entrepreneurship for Productivity: continuous improvement of a technology tool to represent needs in the environment.",
  ]} />

  <Module.Standards items={[

    "2026 CSTA PK-12 · Middle school: algorithms and design, programming with sequences/loops, reading code, and test-based refinement.",
    "NGSS MS-ETS1-1, MS-ETS1-3, and MS-ETS1-4: defines criteria, analyzes test data, and optimizes the drawing robot design.",
  ]} />

    "Calculates or adjusts distance and turns using before/after measurement evidence.",
