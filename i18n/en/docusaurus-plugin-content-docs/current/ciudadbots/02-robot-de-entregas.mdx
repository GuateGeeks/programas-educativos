# 02 · Delivery Bot

:::note For the teacher
Review the **Implementation** tab first (phases and concepts), prepare the **Resources** (base
program and guide), then close out planning with **CNB & Standards** and **Evaluation**. You can
rotate team roles — Builder, Organizer, Programmer — throughout the module.
:::

<Module id="m2">
  <Module.Question>
    How do we design a robot that safely delivers a package in a neighborhood, village, or school
    campus?
  </Module.Question>

  <Module.Context>
    The robot simulates a local GuateGeeks delivery service: bringing medicine, a spare part,
    books, or materials to the right person. It works with autonomous movement, subroutines,
    opening/closing a compartment, buttons, the hub display, and access logic.
  </Module.Context>

  <Module.Concepts items={[
    "Subroutines",
    "Opening and closing with a motor",
    "Route sequences",
    "Buttons and PIN",
    "Secure delivery design",
  ]} />

  <Module.Phase kind="act" label="Activate · 15 min" title="Robots in delivery services">
    Talk about food orders, medicine, books, and school materials. Each team defines who receives
    the package, which route the robot will follow, and which risk it must avoid: wrong delivery,
    obstacle, or unauthorized access. Initial evidence: a mini map with destination, stop point, and
    a safety rule for opening the compartment.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="exp" label="Explore · 25 min" title="Compartment and mechanism">
    Analyze the large motor, medium motors, lid, and crank mechanism. Test opening and closing by
    hand before programming, using a light payload and without forcing gears. Checkpoint: the lid
    moves freely, the package fits without binding, and the team can explain what would happen if
    the motor tries to open while something blocks the lid.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="cre" label="Create · 60-90 min" title="Route with access control">
    Build the Delivery Bot and create subroutines to open, close, turn left, turn right, and move
    forward. Test each route segment first, then integrate a button-based PIN sequence before
    opening. Record in a table whether the robot reached the correct point, accepted the expected
    PIN, and opened/closed without jamming. If it fails, debug the route, PIN, and mechanism
    separately.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="ref" label="Reflect · 15 min" title="Safety and user experience">
    Each group demonstrates a delivery and explains how they would avoid wrong deliveries, what they
    would do if the robot encounters an obstacle, and what information they would show on the hub.
    To scale the challenge, 1st básico runs a guided route, 2nd básico compares two routes with test
    data, and 3rd básico designs a response when the PIN, route, or compartment fails.
  </Module.Phase>
