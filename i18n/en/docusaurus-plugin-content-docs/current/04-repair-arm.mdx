# 04 · Repair Arm

:::note For the teacher
Review the **Implementation** tab first (phases and concepts), prepare the **Resources** (base
program and guide), then close out planning with **CNB & Standards** and **Evaluation**. You can
rotate team roles — Builder, Organizer, Programmer — throughout the module.
:::

<Module id="m4">
  <Module.Question>
    How can a robot help repair a water pipe without putting a person at risk?
  </Module.Question>

  <Module.Context>
    The mission simulates repairing water infrastructure in a community. It introduces a color
    sensor, reference marks, a manipulator arm, gripping, and part replacement.
  </Module.Context>

  <Module.Concepts items={[
    "Color sensor",
    "Black mark as a signal",
    "Manipulator and gripper",
    "Urban maintenance",
    "Collaborative work between robots",
  ]} />

  <Module.Phase kind="act" label="Activate · 12 min" title="Water infrastructure">
    Talk about pipes, drinking water, and repair risks. Why does it make sense to send a robot?
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="exp" label="Explore · 25 min" title="Detection and arm">
    Review the color sensor, the arm motor, and the gripper. Test how the reading changes with
    different colors.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="cre" label="Create · 70-100 min" title="Remove and replace">
    Build the Repair Arm. Program it to move forward to the black mark, stop, pick up the damaged
    part, back up, and place the new part.
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Phase kind="ref" label="Reflect · 15 min" title="Sensors as decisions">
    Distinguish between moving for a set time and moving until something is detected. Which is more
    robust, and why?
  </Module.Phase>

  <Module.Cnb items={[

  ]} />
</Module>
