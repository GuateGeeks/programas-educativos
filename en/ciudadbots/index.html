CiudadBots Guatemala

A teaching path for working through applied robotics with urban-engineering challenges. Here you'll find the program sequence, resources for each module, and the guide for bringing each experience into the classroom: implementation, tracking, and evaluation all in one place.

Designed for grades 7, 8, and 9​

The program uses the same projects as a base, but the expectation changes by grade: grade 7 measures guided comprehension and execution; grade 8 measures analysis, adjustment, and debugging; grade 9 measures original design, technical documentation, and community impact.

Grade 7 · Guided exploration

Goal: move the student from user to first-time builder/programmer.

  • Recognizes components: hub, motor, sensor, and mechanism.
  • Follows build instructions working in roles.
  • Runs simple sequences and loops.
  • Explains cause and effect: block, movement, sensor.

Grade 8 · Analysis and improvement

Goal: the student measures, compares, and improves with evidence.

  • Logs test data and errors.
  • Adjusts degrees, timing, distances, or thresholds.
  • Uses conditionals, subroutines, and sensors with intent.
  • Relates mechanical design to performance.

Grade 9 · Design and impact

Goal: the student proposes their own solutions connected to a real community.

  • Redesigns part of the robot or mission.
  • Documents the algorithm, decisions, and tests.
  • Evaluates safety, efficiency, and social impact.
  • Presents a technical solution with evidence.

Team work profiles​

Organize teams with three rotating profiles:

  • Builder — assembles the robot and compares the structure with the visual guide.
  • Organizer — takes care of materials, coordinates timing, and verifies each step is closed out.
  • Programmer — opens the base program, adjusts blocks, and explains how the code relates to the robot's behavior.

The recommendation is to rotate profiles during the module so everyone explores a different responsibility.

Curriculum framework​

Beyond the lower-secondary CNB, the program lets you report evidence in Mathematics, Natural Sciences, Learning and Communication Technologies, Communication and Language, Spanish, and Entrepreneurship for Productivity. It also connects to international technology, computer science, and engineering standards: ISTE Standards for Students, 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, and NGSS MS-ETS1 Engineering Design. Evidence shouldn't just be "the robot worked" — it should include code, test data, a technical explanation, and design improvement.
