## Context

GuateGeeks has a published Docusaurus hub for educational programs and a complete first program, CiudadBots Guatemala. The existing product surface is strongest for teachers: it contains a program overview, twelve robotics modules, downloadable LEGO SPIKE programs, CNB/international standards alignment, evaluation rubrics, a student build view, and a final showcase.

The business goal is broader than publishing educational content. GuateGeeks needs to become a repeatable institutional product: schools and partners should be able to see the offer, understand how it is implemented, choose a package, use GuateGeeks-owned equipment if needed, receive teacher support, and obtain evidence that helps them justify renewal or expansion.

The near-term constraint is speed. October brings new project opportunities, so the team should not attempt to build a full LMS, payment system, or private portal immediately. The first step is to define the product system, package a paid pilot around CiudadBots, and create the sales/evidence assets needed to validate demand.

## Product Thesis

GuateGeeks should not sell "robotics, AI, drones, VR, and 3D printing" as separate activities. It should sell institutional capability:

> GuateGeeks helps educational institutions implement technology programs with ready-to-use content, teacher guidance, student experiences, physical equipment options, measurable evidence, and final showcases.

The customer buys confidence, differentiation, and visible outcomes. The technology is the medium.

## Product Ladder

### Exploradores

Exploradores is the low-risk entry tier. It introduces students and institutions to applied technology without requiring a major equipment purchase or long-term commitment.

- Status: conceptual/fictitious until validated.
- Ideal buyer: schools curious about technology programs but not ready for a semester commitment.
- Duration: 4 to 6 sessions.
- Format: guided experiences, mini challenges, rotating technology stations, or a short thematic pilot.
- Technologies: robotics basics, AI literacy, electronics, digital citizenship, 3D design, drones, or VR/AR.
- Outcome: a simple visible product, participation evidence, and a recommendation for the next tier.

### Constructores

Constructores is the core structured program tier. CiudadBots is the first real product in this tier.

- Status: real product foundation exists.
- Ideal buyer: schools that want a formal robotics or STEAM program.
- Duration: 12, 24, or 36+ sessions.
- Format: sequenced modules with teacher guide, student view, rubrics, resources, and final showcase.
- Technologies: LEGO SPIKE / robotics, sensors, engineering design, computational thinking.
- Outcome: student teams build, test, improve, document, and present robotics missions.

### Creadores

Creadores is the premium innovation tier. It should position students as designers of original solutions connected to local needs.

- Status: conceptual/fictitious until validated.
- Ideal buyer: schools seeking strong differentiation, capstone programs, innovation fairs, or sponsor-backed impact.
- Duration: semester or annual.
- Format: problem discovery, prototyping, iteration, documentation, and public presentation.
- Technologies: robotics, AI, IoT, 3D printing, electronics, drones, data, and digital storytelling.
- Outcome: original prototypes, impact narratives, portfolio artifacts, and public showcase.

## Offer Architecture

### Demo Experience

Purpose: open doors and show potential quickly.

- 1 session.
- Delivered by GuateGeeks.
- Uses GuateGeeks equipment.
- Includes short director/coordinator debrief.
- Converts into paid pilot.

### Paid Institutional Pilot

Purpose: validate adoption and produce evidence before a larger sale.

- 4 weeks / 4 to 6 sessions.
- One selected CiudadBots module or mini sequence.
- Teacher onboarding.
- Student view and guided materials.
- GuateGeeks equipment rental or full-service delivery when needed.
- Mini showcase.
- Pilot evidence report.

### Semester Program

Purpose: deliver a serious Constructores implementation.

- 12 to 24 sessions.
- Selected CiudadBots sequence.
- Teacher onboarding and check-ins.
- Rubrics, student evidence, and final showcase.
- Optional equipment rental or kit sale.
- End-of-cycle report and renewal recommendation.

### Annual Program

Purpose: become a strategic program partner.

- 36+ sessions.
- Full CiudadBots path plus extension challenges.
- Multiple grade expectations.
- Teacher enablement.
- Student portfolio.
- Showcase for parents/community.
- Report for leadership and sponsors.

## Hardware And Service Model

GuateGeeks physical equipment is a strategic advantage, but it must not define the whole business. The offer should support four equipment paths:

1. **Use your own equipment:** the institution already has compatible robotics kits.
2. **Rent from GuateGeeks:** GuateGeeks provides equipment for pilots or cycles.
3. **Buy a recommended kit:** GuateGeeks recommends or sells a kit package.
4. **Full-service implementation:** GuateGeeks brings equipment, facilitators, setup, and support.

Each path needs operational checklists: inventory, transport, setup, maintenance, responsibility, damage policy, consumables, and storage.

## Platform Model

The Docusaurus site remains the content and marketing foundation. It should evolve into four surfaces:

- Public hub: product positioning, program ladder, proof, and calls to action.
- Public sample: selected modules or previews that prove quality.
- Teacher surface: implementation guide, resources, rubrics, pacing, and evidence templates.
- Student surface: clean step-by-step guides and activity instructions.

Access control should not be built in the first iteration. The first commercial validation can use protected links, manual onboarding, PDFs, or controlled distribution before investing in accounts and payments.

## Evidence System

Evidence is what turns a technology activity into an institutional product. Every pilot and program should produce:

- Attendance/session log.
- Photos or short clips of student work.
- Student/team artifact: robot, code, diagram, bitacora, test table, or presentation.
- Rubric scores for construction, logic, engineering process, collaboration, and communication.
- Teacher feedback.
- Student reflection.
- Director-facing summary.
- Parent/sponsor-facing showcase output.
- Recommendation for next step.

The default evidence artifact should be a concise "GuateGeeks Impact Report" for each pilot or cycle.

## Sales Motion

The first sales motion should be founder-led and institutional.

1. Identify 20 target institutions.
2. Send a concise message focused on institutional outcomes, not technologies.
3. Offer a 20-minute discovery call.
4. Show the hub, CiudadBots, student view, and physical kit.
5. Propose a paid pilot.
6. Execute the pilot and collect evidence.
7. Present the evidence report and upsell semester/annual program.

Initial priority segments:

- Private schools with innovation or STEAM appetite.
- Schools with existing robotics equipment that is underused.
- NGOs or foundations funding education/technology.
- Companies with CSR/ESG education initiatives.

## Communication Strategy

The brand message should be practical and institutional:

> Technology programs ready for the classroom, with teacher support and visible learning evidence.

Avoid leading with a list of technologies. Lead with pain and outcome:

- "Your school wants innovation, but teachers need structure."
- "Students should not only use technology; they should build, explain, and improve solutions."
- "We provide the content, equipment options, teacher guidance, and evidence."

Required assets:

- One-page institutional brochure.
- 8-10 slide sales deck.
- CiudadBots landing page or product section.
- Pilot proposal template.
- Demo agenda.
- WhatsApp/email outreach script.
- Evidence report template.
- Case study template after first client.

## 90-Day Roadmap

### Weeks 1-2: Product Definition

- Finalize product ladder.
- Finalize CiudadBots institutional offer.
- Define pilot package and pricing assumptions.
- Define equipment options.
- Create OpenSpec artifacts and task backlog.

### Weeks 3-4: Sales Assets

- Create institutional landing structure.
- Create brochure and sales deck.
- Create pilot proposal template.
- Create demo script and discovery questions.
- Prepare equipment demo kit checklist.

### Weeks 5-6: Delivery Assets

- Create teacher onboarding flow.
- Create session planning template.
- Create evidence templates.
- Create report template.
- Select pilot modules and implementation sequence.

### Weeks 7-8: Pilot Execution

- Run pilot with current client.
- Capture student evidence.
- Collect teacher/director feedback.
- Note operational friction with equipment and facilitation.

### Weeks 9-10: Proof And Refinement

- Produce pilot report.
- Create first case study.
- Refine package and pricing.
- Improve onboarding and evidence process.

### Weeks 11-12: Acquisition Push

- Contact 20 target institutions.
- Book discovery/demo meetings.
- Sell 3 to 5 pilots.
- Use first client as proof where permission allows.

## Non-Goals For The First Iteration

- Full LMS.
- Authentication and payment system.
- Complete private membership portal.
- Fully built Exploradores and Creadores programs.
- Automated certificate generation.
- Large-scale franchise or facilitator network.
